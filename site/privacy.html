Legal

ONSET privacy policy

Effective date: 31 October 2026

ONSET is a free, open-source (AGPL-3.0-or-later) Twitch client for Android (package app.onset). It is an independent project, not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Twitch Interactive, Inc.

This policy describes what the ONSET app does with data. It is based on the app's source code, which anyone can read at https://github.com/JLmehdi92/onset.

In short

  • ONSET has no account system of its own. It talks directly to Twitch and to several third-party services listed below. Its only server is a small token service that gives logged-out viewers an anonymous Twitch token; it stores nothing about you (section 4).
  • ONSET shows no ads of its own, and the developer does not sell or rent any data.
  • Your Twitch credentials and your history stay on your device; none of it is sent anywhere except to Twitch.
  • The builds published on GitHub include Google Firebase, but send no crash reports, analytics, or performance data unless you turn on Share crash logs and analytics, which is off by default (section 5).

1. Who is responsible

ONSET is developed by Mehdi Seddiki, a private individual based in France (contact: contact@onsetapp.app). The developer is responsible for the app, for its token service (section 4) and for the crash reports and analytics collected through Google Firebase (section 5). Unless you turn on Share crash logs and analytics, the published builds send no crash reports, analytics, or performance data (the Firebase libraries are still started and may contact Google's servers, but without any of that data; see section 5). The token service sees your IP address only briefly (section 4).

Twitch, the emote services, and the other services listed in section 4 decide for themselves what they do with the data they receive. Their own privacy policies apply to it.

2. Logging in

Logging in is optional: you can browse and watch without an account.

When you log in, ONSET opens Twitch's own login and authorization page (id.twitch.tv) inside the app. You type your password into Twitch's page, not into ONSET; ONSET never sees it. Twitch then gives ONSET an access token for the permissions shown on that page (reading and sending chat, your followed channels, blocked users, chat color, moderation actions if you are a moderator, whispers, your emotes, and creating clips).

Because the login page signs you in to twitch.tv, it also leaves a twitch.tv session cookie (auth-token) in the app's web view. On Android, ONSET reads that cookie and keeps it as a "web token" (you can also paste or clear it in Settings › Video › Twitch web token (2K)). It is sent only to Twitch, to use features that Twitch's public API does not offer: higher-quality video renditions, subscriber-only past broadcasts, your watch positions and "Continue watching", followed categories, watch streaks, channel points and predictions, and some real-time events. To keep it valid, ONSET occasionally loads twitch.tv in a hidden web view while you are logged in.

When you are not logged in, ONSET uses an anonymous app token that Twitch issues to the app itself; it is not linked to you. The app gets it from ONSET's token service (section 4), so that the Twitch application secret never has to be inside the app.

5. Crash reports and analytics (Firebase)

The builds published in the ONSET GitHub releases include Google Firebase: Crashlytics (crash reports), Google Analytics for Firebase (usage statistics), and Performance Monitoring (speed measurements). They collect only if you turn on Share crash logs and analytics.

Off by default. The setting is off on a new install. You can turn it on in the setup screens at first launch or in Settings › Other › Privacy, and turn it off at any time in the same place. Reset settings (Settings › Other) turns it off again. Debug builds never collect, whatever the setting.

While it is off, no crash reports, analytics, or performance data are sent. When the app opens, the Firebase libraries are still started and may contact Google's servers (for example to register the installation), without any of that data; the background go-live check does not start them at all. The app hands no error, log line, or measurement to Crashlytics or Performance Monitoring. All three collections are declared disabled until the app applies your choice at startup, so Analytics records nothing either. Crashlytics normally keeps crash reports on the device to send later. With the setting off, the app hands it nothing, and any report Crashlytics stored on its own (for a crash in the app's Android code, for example) is deleted unsent each time the app starts and whenever you turn the setting off. Data sent while the setting was on is not recalled (section 7 says how long it is kept).

While it is on, the app sends (and so does the background go-live check, for its own errors):

  • Crash reports (Crashlytics): crashes and uncaught errors, and some unexpected errors the app recovers from (for example a server error, a chat or real-time connection error, or a video player failure). Each report holds the error's type and message, the stack trace, and a short label of where it happened; for a failed request, its method and the service's address cut to the domain (for example https://api.twitch.tv), never the path or the query. Short log lines are added on some failures (chat and login reconnection attempts; a Twitch directory or highlights query that stopped working, with its error message), and a yes/no value for whether you are logged in. Crashlytics adds technical details such as the device model, Android version, app version, orientation, free memory, and storage.
  • Analytics (Google Analytics for Firebase): the name of each app screen you open. These are fixed names such as VideoChat, VodScreen, Settings, or /search, never the channel, video, or search behind them. ONSET logs no other events of its own. The Analytics library also records events by itself (such as first open, sessions, time spent in the app, and app updates) with device and app details (device model, Android version, app version, and language) and an approximate location (country or region) that Google derives from your IP address.
  • Performance (Performance Monitoring): for each request the app makes through its own HTTP client, the service's domain only (no path or query), the method, the response code, and the duration. The Performance library also measures some things by itself, such as the app's startup time and screen rendering (slow or frozen frames). Google also receives your IP address, which it uses to find the country, and the Firebase installation ID.

What is not included. ONSET puts no Twitch user ID, username, or channel name in these reports, and no tokens. Request addresses are cut to the domain. The Firebase libraries tell installations apart with random identifiers they generate (a Firebase installation ID and an Analytics app instance ID), which are not linked to your Twitch account and are replaced when you clear the app's data or reinstall it. The advertising ID is not collected: the app removes the advertising ID permissions, turns off advertising ID collection in Analytics and disables ad personalization signals.

Legal basis. Your consent (GDPR Article 6(1)(a)), given by turning the setting on. You can withdraw it at any time by turning the setting off; this does not affect what was collected before.

Google as processor. Google processes this data as the developer's processor, under the Firebase data processing terms (Analytics data sharing with Google is turned off in the project), and may process it outside the European Union, including in the United States, with the safeguards those terms provide.

7. How long data is kept

  • On your device: until you log out (tokens, stored login, and web cookies), clear the image cache, clear the app's data, or uninstall the app (everything else, including the Twitch user ID kept with local lists). Lists are capped as described in section 3.
  • Firebase (only what was sent while Share crash logs and analytics was on): Crashlytics keeps crash data and its identifiers for 90 days. Performance Monitoring keeps events linked to an IP address for 30 days and other performance data for 60 days. Google Analytics keeps user- and event-level data for 2 months (the project's setting); aggregated, non-identifying reports are not affected by that limit. The Firebase installation ID is kept until the developer deletes it.
  • Twitch and other services: according to their own policies.

8. Deleting your data

  • Log out (Profile or Settings): ONSET asks Twitch to revoke your access token, deletes your tokens and the stored login (your Twitch user ID in the encrypted storage) from the device, and clears the web view's cookies. Your settings and local history stay on the device, and so does the Twitch user ID kept with those local lists (watch positions, accepted chat rules, and the go-live check) until you clear the app's data or uninstall it. ONSET does not ask Twitch to end the twitch.tv web session itself. To remove ONSET's access to your account, use your Twitch settings, under Connections.
  • Clear the app's data (Android Settings › Apps › ONSET › Storage) or uninstall ONSET: everything ONSET stored on the device is deleted, including unsent crash reports and the Firebase identifiers.
  • Turn off Share crash logs and analytics (Settings › Other › Privacy): no more crash reports, analytics, or performance data are sent, and the crash reports kept on the device and not yet sent are deleted. What Firebase already received carries no account identifier (no Twitch user ID or username, only the random identifiers of section 5), so the developer may be unable to match an emailed deletion request to your data. You can still write to contact@onsetapp.app; in any case that data expires with the retention periods of section 7.
  • Data held by Twitch and the other services is deleted through them.

9. Children

ONSET is a client for Twitch and is meant for people who are allowed to use Twitch. Twitch's Terms of Service require users to be at least 13 years old (or older where local law sets a higher minimum age), and users under the age of majority may use Twitch only with a parent's or guardian's supervision and agreement. You must meet Twitch's age requirements to use ONSET. ONSET is not directed at children under 13.

10. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access, correct, and delete your personal data, to object to or restrict its processing, and to data portability, and you can withdraw your consent to crash reports and analytics at any time by turning off Share crash logs and analytics. The developer holds no data about you from the published builds unless you turned that setting on; that data is linked to no account, so the developer may be unable to find yours (sections 5 and 8). To exercise a right, or for any question, write to contact@onsetapp.app. You can also lodge a complaint with the French data protection authority, the CNIL (https://www.cnil.fr).

For data held by Twitch or the other services, contact them directly.
